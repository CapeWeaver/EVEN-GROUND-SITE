SIYAKWAZI

Ready for school, in her own way

In South Africa, an estimated half a million children with disabilities are not in school at all.

Our partner Siyakwazi is working to change that in rural KwaZulu-Natal: with therapy for children, support for their families, and training for the preschools that welcome them. Isenathi is one of the children it supports.

She is almost five and lives with cerebral palsy. With her family and Siyakwazi beside her, she has gone from learning to sit to starting preschool.

That didn’t happen by accident. When Isenathi was just a few months old, a worker at her clinic referred her mom to Siyakwazi, so her support started early. Her mom took part from the very beginning, with her uncle and her gogo (grandmother) alongside.

Siyakwazi’s therapists started with the basics: an adaptive chair to help her sit up straight, which she could do by around 18 months, and a stand frame to help her stand. Very quickly she was pulling herself up, then walking with a little trolley. Soon she didn’t need the trolley at all.

“Her gross motor skills were the quickest to develop,” says her therapist.

“But the biggest change in Isenathi is her social communication. She’s so bubbly and bright, talks so beautifully, and engages with people.”

“If someone comes to help Isenathi, I just open my arms,” says her mom. “I want the best for her.”

Her mom joined Siyakwazi’s parent support group, which helped her too.

“Sometimes you think your problems are huge. Then you hear from others facing more, and still carrying on with life.”

1 / 10

Her uncle joined the Siyakwazi sewing hub, where he makes learning resources for other children and earns an income for the household. At home, her mom and gogo made sure she knew her body parts and basic concepts, a strong foundation for school.

The changes reach the whole household. Isenathi used to cry when she was hungry, so only her mom or gogo could care for her. Now she can say she’s hungry, so her uncles can look after her too.

This year Isenathi started preschool, where her teachers give her the extra support she needs in the classroom. She comes home singing the songs she learns there.

“She is doing really well at school,” says her uncle. “She writes now, and she knows all the songs.”

One child. A whole family lifted.

Siyakwazi’s work goes beyond therapy and school readiness. It supports parents, and helps preschools welcome children with disabilities. In 2025, it supported 170 children with disabilities and their families.

“Everything would be difficult if Siyakwazi wasn’t there for Isenathi,” her uncle says.

“Everything the other children do, she does too”

When Isenathi arrived at Khulanathi Day Care, her teacher wasn’t sure she could join in. “I never studied to work with children like Isenathi,” she says. Siyakwazi trained her, and she learned how to include Isenathi in everything the class does.

At first, Isenathi fell often, and her teacher rushed to help every time. Slowly she learned to step back, and Isenathi grew more confident.

Today Isenathi plays with the toy train, joins in the rhymes, swings without needing a push and climbs the slide ladder on her own. In color games she matches a green card to something “green like grass,” just like the rest of her group.

“Everything done by other children,” her teacher says, “she does too.”
