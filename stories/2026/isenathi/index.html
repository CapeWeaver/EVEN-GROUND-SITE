When Isenathi first came to Siyakwazi, she couldn't crawl.

Siyakwazi · November 9, 2026

  • Isenathi(11/09/2026)

This year, a lot of the help that reached South African families disappeared. Here's what your support kept going.

She is almost five and lives with cerebral palsy. Our therapists started with the basics: sitting, then crawling. Within months she was pulling herself up to stand. Then she was walking with a little trolley. Then she didn't need the trolley.

Donate now

"Her gross motor skills developed the fastest," says her therapist. "But the biggest change in Isenathi is her social communication. She's so bubbly and bright, talks so beautifully, and engages with people."

1 / 11

That didn't happen by accident. Her mom, her uncle and her gogo came to every session. Her mom joined our parent support group. Her uncle joined the Siyakwazi sewing hub, where he makes learning resources for other children and earns an income for the household. Isenathi started creche this year, and her practitioners give her the classroom support she needs to keep up.

One child. A whole family lifted.

As US aid pulls back, the gap for families like hers keeps growing. Siyakwazi is still here because you are. Your gift pays for the therapy, the parent support and the classroom help that turned a child who couldn't crawl into a confident little girl ready for school.Team flag: "Your gift pays for the therapy" implies a gift designated to Siyakwazi (per-partner giving was removed 2026-10-13).

There are more children like Isenathi waiting for their first session.
