In South Africa, more than one in three people is a child. For millions of them, the chance of a good education is shaped before they are even born, by inequalities that still run deep.

South Africa’s leaders have recognised that a child’s earliest years matter most. Yet about one million children aged three to five still don’t have access to any early learning programs before they go to primary school.

This year, deep cuts to US foreign aid have left many programs for children and families across Africa without funding. That makes community-led organizations, and the people who stand behind them, more important than ever.

8 in 10 By age ten, eight in ten children can’t read with understanding in any language.

At age four, fewer than half of children in early learning programs are on track to start school ready to learn. For children with no access to preschool at all, it’s fewer than one in five.

Those numbers aren’t a verdict on the children. They reflect what was, and wasn’t, around them: a dedicated early childhood teacher who prepared them for school, a trained teacher, a classroom that makes room for every kind of learner.

Three children, three stories

That’s why, this fall, we’re introducing you to three children, each supported by one of our partners. You’ll learn who they are, what has changed for them, and what they’re working toward now.

Each comes from a different kind of program: early learning and inclusion for children with disabilities (Siyakwazi), support for early childhood centers in informal settlements (True North), and a low-fee independent school (Kgololo Academy). Together, they show how our partners are leveling the playing field for children across South Africa.

Siyakwazi

Isenathi

A child with a disability who has found her place in an inclusive classroom in rural KwaZulu-Natal.

Siyakwazi Inclusion, rehabilitation therapy, school readiness and early learning for children with disabilities in rural KwaZulu-Natal. 170 children with disabilities and their families supported in 2025, and 7,000+ home visits through the Inclusion and Early Learning programs.

True North

Melokuhle

A first-grader on her way to school, supported by a holistic early childhood center in Vrygrond, Cape Town.

True North Mentorship for early childhood teachers and principals, and holistic child development, in Vrygrond, Cape Town. True North strengthens early childhood education by training, mentoring and equipping 30 preschools and 144 staff who care for more than 1,700 young children each day.

Kgololo Academy

Lesego

A student in Alexandra, Johannesburg, who plans to become a defense lawyer.

Kgololo Academy A low-fee independent primary school that aims to give children in Alexandra, Johannesburg, a world-class education within their own community. 236 students in 2025. 90% of Grade 7 learners awarded fully funded places at leading high schools.
